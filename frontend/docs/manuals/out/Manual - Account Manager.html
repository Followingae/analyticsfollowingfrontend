03 · Urgent or not

Clients will call many things urgent

Ask one question. The answer sets the reply time, and nothing else does.

The one question
Will a visit or a post suffer if this waits until 09:00 tomorrow?
A new request arrives in the client group. Whoever reads it answers it, then asks this.
Yes
Urgent
Drop planned work. Reply in 15 to 30 minutes.
  • A creator is at the store and something is wrong
  • A mistake on a post that is live
  • Tomorrow’s post or visit is blocked
  • A creator is unreachable on a live day
  • Wrong claim, wrong product, bad comments
No
Not urgent
Reply in 15 minutes with a time. Deliver within our times.
  • A new shortlist or more options
  • Scripts, changes, new ideas
  • Reports, insights, screenshots
  • Changes to next week’s plan
  • Invoices and payments, by email
Then log it, the same day

Every client request is raised on the platform as a Request the same day, whoever answers it. Answer the client first, as a person. Then log it, so somebody owns it and a date exists.

15 · The wall

The TV in the office

It shows what the platform knows, about a second after it knows it. Nobody writes on it.

What it shows
  • Every live campaign as a row: who is briefed, who has content in, who has posted.
  • Whose move it is, with the faces. Yours is lit when it is you.
  • A celebration when a client confirms, a post goes live or a deal is won.
  • Everything else floats up for ten seconds and is gone.
What puts it there
  • You do. A rung moved, a link pasted, a request closed, a rate agreed.
  • Nothing is typed onto the wall and nobody can push to it. There is no button.
  • A thing that happened in WhatsApp and nowhere else does not exist to the wall.
  • If the room should see it, record it. The wall moves the same second.
Never money
  • No fee, cost, margin or invoice on a team display.
  • Business development and the founders’ own work stay off it.
  • The wall is for the people delivering campaigns.
Pairing a TVThe TV opens following.ae/tv and shows a six-digit code. A staff phone enters it and picks the display. The TV remembers it through power off and on.

04 · Your jobs

What you actually do

In order. The strip on each card is what to click, as you click it.

1

Turn a client ask into something that happens

Every time a client asks for anything.
Go toRequestsRaise one
  1. Write what they asked for in their words, not a summary.
  2. Tick "a client asked for this" so it carries the weight it should.
  3. Give it a date. Leave the owner blank if you do not know who should do it.
  4. It shows on their day until it is done, and they are told when it is.
Next: You stop being the only person who remembers it.
2

Keep the campaign true

Every time a creator moves: briefed, content in, approved, posted.
Go toCampaignsOpen a campaignDelivery
  1. Move the creator’s rung the day it happens, not on Friday.
  2. A post that is live gets its link on the row. The client’s results read from it.
  3. A creator the client agreed who is not in our database yet: add them by hand, with their handle.
  4. Your clients are the ones with your name on the client record. That is what fills your Today.
Next: Talent chase from the same ladder, and the office wall shows it the moment it moves.
3

Send a client their results

Once their creators have posted.
Go toCampaignsOpen a campaignPerformance
  1. Paste the link to each post that went live.
  2. We read the numbers off Instagram within a minute or so.
  3. Leadership releases each one. Nothing reaches the client until they do.
  4. Once released the client sees it on their own Results screen.
Next: They get the roll-up across every post without you building a deck.
4

Take the handover

When business development closes a brand and hands it to you.
Go toClientsBrands
  1. Read what was agreed: rate card, deliverables, dates, who signs.
  2. If any of it is only in an email, put it on the client record.
  3. Introduce yourself to the client in writing on day one.
Next: You now own this brand. Everything about them comes to you.
5

Review the content

When a creator’s row on Delivery reads "Content in".
Go toCampaignsOpen a campaignContent
  1. Check it against the brief before the client sees anything.
  2. With Frame.io, press Send for review. The client answers inside our platform.
  3. Changes asked for go back to the creator through talent, with the note.
Next: Approved content moves the creator to "get the post link".
6

Follow the proposal, never send it

Once the Founder & CEO has sent the client a proposal or a quote.
Go toClientsQuotes
  1. Only the Founder & CEO sends a proposal or a quote to a client. You never do.
  2. Not from the platform, and not from your own inbox. The platform never emails a client.
  3. The client picks in the proposal. You see what they chose without asking.
  4. If they tell you instead, pass it on. The Founder & CEO uses Confirm for the client.
Next: Confirming locks the creators they took. More later means Reopen, not a new proposal.
7

Report on the campaign

During delivery, and again at the end.
Go toCampaignsReports
  1. Share what is live as it happens, not only at the end.
  2. Send the write-up when the campaign finishes.
  3. Nothing goes to a client automatically. You press send.

06 · Right and wrong

What good looks like

Everything on the right has actually happened here and cost us something.

What good looks like
  • The client hears from you on WhatsApp within the hour, as always.
  • Then it is raised as a request, with a date, so it is not only in your head.
  • Every promise you make goes on the client record the same day.
  • You chase from the Delivery tab, which shows who is actually late.
  • Results reach the client as a grid they can read, not a deck you built at midnight.
What has actually gone wrong
  • Answering on WhatsApp and nothing else. You become the only person who knows, and the only person who can be ill.
  • Chasing from memory. The one you forget is always the one the client remembers.
  • Telling a client a date that talent has not agreed to.
  • Sending content to a client before it has been checked internally.
  • Building results by hand in a spreadsheet when the platform has already counted them.
07 · The hard rules

Rules that cost money

Each one has already cost us something.

  • You never see what a creator costs us. You sell. That is deliberate.
  • A brand’s brief that reaches you goes to the Founder & CEO the same day. You never set one up or send a proposal or quote.
  • No automated email ever reaches your client. Every one is sent by a person.
  • A promise made in email belongs on the client record the same day.
  • If a brand has gone quiet for a fortnight you will be told. Act on it.
  • The status sheet is retired. A creator’s rung lives on the campaign, moved the day it changes.

Every screen in the platform is explained under Guide in the menu. If something here does not match what you see, the product is right and this is out of date. Say so and it gets reprinted.
