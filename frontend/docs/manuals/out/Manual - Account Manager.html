01 · Your day

How a day goes

Three moments. Everything else finds you.

First thing
  • Open Requests. What clients are waiting on, and who owes it.
  • Open Today. The rest of what is yours.
  • Open Clients. Anyone gone quiet is flagged.
Through the day
  • Answer the client wherever they wrote to you. WhatsApp and email do not change.
  • Then raise it as a Request so somebody owns it and a date exists.
  • Every promise you make goes on the client’s record the same day.
  • A client should never be the one chasing us.
Before you log off
  • No client waiting on an answer from you overnight.
  • Anything you promised today is written down.

05 · Your jobs

What you actually do

In order. The strip on each card is what to click, as you click it.

1

Turn a client ask into something that happens

Every time a client asks for anything.
Go toRequestsRaise one
  1. Write what they asked for in their words, not a summary.
  2. Tick "a client asked for this" so it carries the weight it should.
  3. Give it a date. Leave the owner blank if you do not know who should do it.
  4. It shows on their day until it is done, and they are told when it is.
Next: You stop being the only person who remembers it.
2

Send a client their results

Once their creators have posted.
Go toCampaignsOpen a campaignPerformance
  1. Paste the link to each post that went live.
  2. We read the numbers off Instagram within a minute or so.
  3. Leadership releases each one. Nothing reaches the client until they do.
  4. Once released the client sees it on their own Results screen.
Next: They get the roll-up across every post without you building a deck.
3

Take the handover

When business development closes a brand and hands it to you.
Go toClientsBrands
  1. Read what was agreed: rate card, deliverables, dates, who signs.
  2. If any of it is only in an email, put it on the client record.
  3. Introduce yourself to the client in writing on day one.
Next: You now own this brand. Everything about them comes to you.
4

Write the internal brief

Before anybody sources a single creator.
Go toClientsBrandsOpen the brand
  1. Market, category, budget per creator, deliverables, dates.
  2. Say what the brand will refuse. That saves a week of sourcing.
  3. Release it so talent can start.
Next: Talent sources against your brief. A vague brief is a wasted week.
5

Send the roster and get an answer

Once leadership has cleared the list.
Go toCreatorsRostersShare link
  1. Mint the link and send it. The client picks on the page itself.
  2. You are told when they answer. You do not have to check.
  3. If the link is about to expire you are warned first.
Next: Their picks come back onto the roster and become a quote.
6

Report on the campaign

During delivery, and again at the end.
Go toCampaignsReports
  1. Share what is live as it happens, not only at the end.
  2. Send the write-up when the campaign finishes.
  3. Nothing goes to a client automatically. You press send.

07 · Right and wrong

What good looks like

Everything on the right has actually happened here and cost us something.

What good looks like
  • The client hears from you on WhatsApp within the hour, as always.
  • Then it is raised as a request, with a date, so it is not only in your head.
  • Every promise you make goes on the client record the same day.
  • You chase from the Delivery tab, which shows who is actually late.
  • Results reach the client as a grid they can read, not a deck you built at midnight.
What has actually gone wrong
  • Answering on WhatsApp and nothing else. You become the only person who knows, and the only person who can be ill.
  • Chasing from memory. The one you forget is always the one the client remembers.
  • Telling a client a date that talent has not agreed to.
  • Sending content to a client before it has been checked internally.
  • Building results by hand in a spreadsheet when the platform has already counted them.
08 · Vocabulary

Words we use

They mean something specific. A roster is not a list, and cost is not sell.

Brand
A company we are talking to. Becomes a client when a deal is won.
Client
A brand that has bought something. They have an account and can log in.
Deal
One conversation about money. A brand can have many, over years.
Campaign
A deal that was won and opened. Real creators, real money.
Roster
The creators sourced for one client. Has rounds, a brief and an owner.
List
A standing question: "fashion, UAE". Answers itself. Never client-specific.
Cost
What we pay a creator. Founders and talent only. Never shown to a brand.
Sell
What we charge a client. A creator with no sell price cannot be sold.
Quoted cost
What we assumed when pricing. Rarely what we end up paying.
Agreed rate
What a creator actually accepted. The only number that is real.
Request
Something somebody is waiting on, with an owner and a date.
Proposal
A priced roster sent to a client to choose from.
Share link
A /p/ link a client opens without logging in. Mint it, then send it.
Enrolment link
How a creator signs. They enter their own bank details; nobody types a fee.
The ladder
The rungs a booked creator climbs: briefed, content in, posted, paid.
Held
Stopped until a founder answers. Nothing has happened yet.
09 · The hard rules

Rules that cost money

Each one has already cost us something.

  • You never see what a creator costs us. You sell. That is deliberate.
  • No automated email ever reaches your client. Every one is sent by a person.
  • A promise made in email belongs on the client record the same day.
  • If a brand has gone quiet for a fortnight you will be told. Act on it.

Every screen in the platform is explained under Guide in the menu. If something here does not match what you see, the product is right and this is out of date. Say so and it gets reprinted.
