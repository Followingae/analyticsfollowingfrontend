01 · Your day

How a day goes

Three moments. Everything else finds you.

First thing
  • Open Requests. Everything anybody is waiting on from you.
  • Open Today. The rest of what is yours.
  • Open Pipeline. Every creator we owe something.
Through the day
  • Work from Pipeline, not from memory or WhatsApp.
  • Each row tells you the verb. Do that one thing.
  • If it only happened in chat, nobody else can see it.
Before you log off
  • Nothing red in Pipeline without a reason written down.
  • Move it, note it, or nudge whoever is blocking it.

05 · Your jobs

What you actually do

In order. The strip on each card is what to click, as you click it.

1

Find creators for a brand

Starts when a brief arrives. Ends when the client has a list to choose from.
Go toCreatorsRosters
  1. The ask arrives in Requests with a brief. Press "I have this".
  2. A roster is already open for that brand. Everything you find goes there.
  3. Start in Lists: Food UAE, Beauty UAE and the rest are always up to date.
  4. Then the master database. Add anyone new by handle and we pull their numbers.
  5. Nobody tells you how many to find. A client signing fifteen may need to see hundreds.
  6. Record what they charge and what they agreed. Those are two different numbers.
  7. Hand the list up for review when it is ready to be seen.
Next: Leadership reviews for quality and budget. Expect removals. That is the point of the review.
2

Keep the pricing queue empty

Every day. It is the shortest job here and the most expensive to skip.
Go toCreatorsPending rates
  1. This queue is literally the creators nobody can sell yet.
  2. A creator with no sell price cannot go on a quote at all.
  3. Add the cost you agreed. Leadership sets what we sell them for.
Next: A founder prices them and releases them into the master database.
3

Sign a creator

Once the client has picked them and the campaign is confirmed.
Go toFinanceEnrolments
  1. Create the enrolment. The creator gets their own signing link.
  2. They fill in their details and bank information themselves.
  3. Never type a creator’s bank details for them.
  4. Chase anyone who has not signed. The screen shows who.
Next: A founder approves the payment once the work is delivered.
4

Get the content in

From the agreed date until the post is live.
Go toCampaignsChasing
  1. This screen is who is late, who is due, who has gone quiet.
  2. Collect the content and the creator’s own insights screenshots.
  3. Put the posted link on the creator’s row when it goes live.
Next: Account management reports it to the client.

07 · Right and wrong

What good looks like

Everything on the right has actually happened here and cost us something.

What good looks like
  • Everything you find goes on the roster, the same hour you find it.
  • A price is recorded as two numbers: what they ask, what they agreed.
  • A creator you cannot place in a country is still worth adding; say so in the note.
  • When a brief is missing market, category or budget, you ask before you start.
  • You take a request the moment you start it, so nobody duplicates your work.
What has actually gone wrong
  • Sourcing into a spreadsheet and uploading at the end. Nobody can see progress, and the half you have done looks like nothing.
  • Recording only the published rate. We then sell at a margin that does not exist.
  • Guessing a country code on a phone number. We once sent a Saudi creator’s signing link to a stranger in the UAE.
  • Agreeing a fee in WhatsApp and typing it in later. The number drifts, every time.
  • Leaving a request unclaimed while you work on it. Somebody else starts it too.
08 · Vocabulary

Words we use

They mean something specific. A roster is not a list, and cost is not sell.

Brand
A company we are talking to. Becomes a client when a deal is won.
Client
A brand that has bought something. They have an account and can log in.
Deal
One conversation about money. A brand can have many, over years.
Campaign
A deal that was won and opened. Real creators, real money.
Roster
The creators sourced for one client. Has rounds, a brief and an owner.
List
A standing question: "fashion, UAE". Answers itself. Never client-specific.
Cost
What we pay a creator. Founders and talent only. Never shown to a brand.
Sell
What we charge a client. A creator with no sell price cannot be sold.
Quoted cost
What we assumed when pricing. Rarely what we end up paying.
Agreed rate
What a creator actually accepted. The only number that is real.
Request
Something somebody is waiting on, with an owner and a date.
Proposal
A priced roster sent to a client to choose from.
Share link
A /p/ link a client opens without logging in. Mint it, then send it.
Enrolment link
How a creator signs. They enter their own bank details; nobody types a fee.
The ladder
The rungs a booked creator climbs: briefed, content in, posted, paid.
Held
Stopped until a founder answers. Nothing has happened yet.
09 · The hard rules

Rules that cost money

Each one has already cost us something.

  • A creator with no sell price cannot be sold. Price them or they do not exist to us.
  • Never guess a creator’s country code. We once sent a Saudi creator’s signing link to a stranger.
  • The agreed rate is the one we use, not their published rate.
  • A creator signs their own paperwork and enters their own bank details.
  • Nothing is real until it is on the screen. WhatsApp is not a record.

Every screen in the platform is explained under Guide in the menu. If something here does not match what you see, the product is right and this is out of date. Say so and it gets reprinted.
