05 · Your jobs

What you actually do

In order. The strip on each card is what to click, as you click it.

1

Decide what we chase

When a brand is logged as worth pursuing.
Go toClientsBrands
  1. A logged brand is not work until you say so.
  2. Write what we are looking for, then release it.
  3. That release is what starts talent researching.
Next: Talent begins sourcing against what you wrote.
2

Agree what we pay each creator

As soon as a client confirms. Nothing opens until this is done.
Go toFinanceRates
  1. Every proposal a client has confirmed is here, longest wait first.
  2. Open one and put the real negotiated cost against each creator.
  3. The margin moves as you type, before you commit anything.
  4. What we quoted and what we pay are rarely the same. The second one is the truth.
  5. When every creator has a rate, the campaign can be opened.
Next: The campaign opens with a real margin instead of acquiring one later.
3

See who is holding what

Whenever you are allocating, and before you hire.
Go toFinanceRates
  1. The talent board is underneath the rates.
  2. Open requests, how many are late, rosters held, sourced and priced this month.
  3. Counts, never scores. It tells you where the load is, not who is better.
  4. Anything nobody has taken is called out at the top.
4

See what a campaign is worth

Before you invoice, and whenever it matters.
Go toCampaignsOpen a campaignMoney
  1. Cost, sell, margin and what the client has actually paid.
  2. How much is left to draw before we are funding it ourselves.
  3. Approve working capital here if creators must be paid first.
5

Invoice and collect

On the agreed schedule.
Go toClientsBrandsOpen the brand
  1. Raise the advance, then the balance on the agreed terms.
  2. Record the invoice and attach the file.
  3. Mark it paid when the transfer lands.
6

See who has contacted the company

Whenever you want to know, not as a queue.
Go toDesk
  1. Everything sent to our catch-all address or the marketing number lands here.
  2. Each one says who we think it is from, and says so plainly when we do not know.
  3. Nobody replies from here and nothing changes because of it. Only you see it.
7

Control what the platform sends

Whenever the noise is wrong.
Go toAdminSettingsSending
  1. Every automated email in the platform is listed here.
  2. Per event: switch it off, or move it into the twice-daily digest.
  3. Choose which roles hear it, including whether you do.
06 · A real one

A post goes live, and a client sees it

One job, followed the whole way, with the real numbers.

Start to finish
Day 1
A creator posts. Account management pastes the link on the campaign’s Performance tab.
Day 1
We read it from Instagram within a minute. Plays, likes, comments, engagement, measured and not estimated.
Day 1
It sits in Waiting on you. The client cannot see it.
Day 1
You release it. Only then does it appear on their Results screen, with the roll-up across every released post.
Weekly
We re-count it every seven days for a month, then stop. The clock is stamped once from the first post so it cannot run forever.

07 · Right and wrong

What good looks like

Everything on the right has actually happened here and cost us something.

What good looks like
  • Answer the queue daily. Everything in it is somebody stopped mid-task.
  • Turn something down with a reason. It stops the same request next week.
  • Release results as they come, so a client sees the campaign working.
  • Check the talent board before hiring, not after.
  • Let the team work in their own lane without asking you.
What has actually gone wrong
  • Letting the queue sit. The gate turns into a reason people work around the platform.
  • Approving without reading. The 202 means nothing has happened yet, and it will happen exactly as submitted.
  • Switching the gate on the day before you travel.
  • Sending a client anything automatically. No automated email ever reaches a client.
08 · Vocabulary

Words we use

They mean something specific. A roster is not a list, and cost is not sell.

Brand
A company we are talking to. Becomes a client when a deal is won.
Client
A brand that has bought something. They have an account and can log in.
Deal
One conversation about money. A brand can have many, over years.
Campaign
A deal that was won and opened. Real creators, real money.
Roster
The creators sourced for one client. Has rounds, a brief and an owner.
List
A standing question: "fashion, UAE". Answers itself. Never client-specific.
Cost
What we pay a creator. Founders and talent only. Never shown to a brand.
Sell
What we charge a client. A creator with no sell price cannot be sold.
Quoted cost
What we assumed when pricing. Rarely what we end up paying.
Agreed rate
What a creator actually accepted. The only number that is real.
Request
Something somebody is waiting on, with an owner and a date.
Proposal
A priced roster sent to a client to choose from.
Share link
A /p/ link a client opens without logging in. Mint it, then send it.
Enrolment link
How a creator signs. They enter their own bank details; nobody types a fee.
The ladder
The rungs a booked creator climbs: briefed, content in, posted, paid.
Held
Stopped until a founder answers. Nothing has happened yet.
09 · The hard rules

Rules that cost money

Each one has already cost us something.

  • No automated email ever reaches a client. Every client email is sent by a person.
  • A campaign is never quietly repriced. Terms are stamped when it is awarded.
  • Nothing pays out beyond what the client has funded, unless you approve it in writing.
  • Cost is never visible to a brand. Only what we sell for.

Every screen in the platform is explained under Guide in the menu. If something here does not match what you see, the product is right and this is out of date. Say so and it gets reprinted.
