01 · Your day

How a day goes

Three moments. Everything else finds you.

First thing
  • Open Rates. Clients have said yes; these campaigns cannot open until you price them.
  • Open Decisions. Everything held for you, oldest first.
Through the day
  • Clear rosters quickly. Talent cannot move until you do.
  • Strike with a reason. The reason stops the repeat next month.
  • Check the talent board on Rates: who is holding what, and what is late.
Before you log off
  • Nothing has waited on you for more than a day.

05 · Your jobs

What you actually do

In order. The strip on each card is what to click, as you click it.

1

Clear a roster

When talent has stocked it to the target.
Go toCreatorsRosters
  1. Check each creator against the brief’s budget.
  2. Check they really live in the market the brief asked for.
  3. Check engagement against follower count.
  4. Check we have not used them for this brand recently.
  5. Clear the ones that pass. Strike the rest with the reason.
Next: Account management sends the cleared list to the client.
2

Price what talent has found

As soon as a cost is recorded.
Go toCreatorsPending rates
  1. Talent records what a creator charges us.
  2. You set what we sell them for. Nobody else can.
  3. Until you do, that creator cannot go on a quote.
3

Release money

When work is delivered and a payment is requested.
Go toFinanceApprovals
  1. Check the work actually landed before approving.
  2. Check the creator has signed and given their own bank details.
  3. Approve. Nothing pays out that the client has not funded.
Next: The payment joins the next payout run.
4

Watch the shape of the week

Once a week is enough.
Go toAdminCompanyTargets
  1. Targets and progress for the whole team.
  2. Quiet brands and overdue rosters reach you without looking.
06 · A real one

Thirteen creators, one afternoon

One job, followed the whole way, with the real numbers.

Thirteen creators, one afternoon
Open
Minimalist confirmed three weeks ago. Thirteen creators, not one priced, and the campaign cannot open.
Why
Nobody was chasing it because it lived inside one proposal’s page. That is what this screen exists to stop.
Price
What we quoted is what we assumed. What we pay is what you negotiate. On the live Lago campaign one creator quoted 6,000 settled at 3,500.
Watch
The margin updates as you type. You see the consequence of a number before you commit it.
Done
Thirteen rates agreed, the campaign opens with a real margin instead of acquiring one later.
Then
Aqsa raises the enrolment links. Each creator signs their own agreement and enters their own bank details. Nobody types a fee.
07 · Right and wrong

What good looks like

Everything on the right has actually happened here and cost us something.

What good looks like
  • Price what clients have already agreed to, before anything else in your day.
  • Clear rosters fast. Talent genuinely cannot move until you do.
  • Strike a creator with a reason written down. The reason stops the repeat next month.
  • Check the talent board when you are allocating, not only when something has gone wrong.
  • Use the quoted cost as a starting point, never as the answer.
What has actually gone wrong
  • Leaving a confirmed proposal unpriced. The client has said yes and is waiting on us.
  • Opening a campaign and agreeing rates afterwards. You are then negotiating with somebody who has already started working.
  • Approving a creator with no sell price. They cannot be sold, so they are not real.
  • Letting a payment go out that the client has not funded.
  • Reading the talent board as a scoreboard. It shows load, not quality.
08 · Vocabulary

Words we use

They mean something specific. A roster is not a list, and cost is not sell.

Brand
A company we are talking to. Becomes a client when a deal is won.
Client
A brand that has bought something. They have an account and can log in.
Deal
One conversation about money. A brand can have many, over years.
Campaign
A deal that was won and opened. Real creators, real money.
Roster
The creators sourced for one client. Has rounds, a brief and an owner.
List
A standing question: "fashion, UAE". Answers itself. Never client-specific.
Cost
What we pay a creator. Founders and talent only. Never shown to a brand.
Sell
What we charge a client. A creator with no sell price cannot be sold.
Quoted cost
What we assumed when pricing. Rarely what we end up paying.
Agreed rate
What a creator actually accepted. The only number that is real.
Request
Something somebody is waiting on, with an owner and a date.
Proposal
A priced roster sent to a client to choose from.
Share link
A /p/ link a client opens without logging in. Mint it, then send it.
Enrolment link
How a creator signs. They enter their own bank details; nobody types a fee.
The ladder
The rungs a booked creator climbs: briefed, content in, posted, paid.
Held
Stopped until a founder answers. Nothing has happened yet.
09 · The hard rules

Rules that cost money

Each one has already cost us something.

  • A creator the client should not see is your catch, not theirs.
  • Always strike with a reason. No reason means it happens again next month.
  • No payment goes out on work you have not confirmed landed.
  • Working capital is an amount you approve in writing, never a general permission.

Every screen in the platform is explained under Guide in the menu. If something here does not match what you see, the product is right and this is out of date. Say so and it gets reprinted.
