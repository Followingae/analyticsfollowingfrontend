05 · Your jobs

What you actually do

In order. The strip on each card is what to click, as you click it.

1

Take the enquiry

The moment it arrives.
Go toClientsPartner leads
  1. Everybody who filled in the form on the website is here, newest first.
  2. Open the one you are taking and write down what they asked for.
  3. Put your name on it so nobody answers it twice.
2

Qualify it

Before you quote anything.
Go toClientsSalesAdd a brand
  1. Budget, market, category, timing, who signs.
  2. Without a budget there is no brief, and no brief wastes everyone.
  3. Add the brand on the board and write what you learned on the deal.
Next: A founder decides whether we chase it and writes what to look for.
3

Ask talent to start

The moment the deal looks real. NOT when it closes.
Go toClientsSalesAsk talent
  1. This is the one that used to cost us weeks. Talent heard when a campaign locked.
  2. Press it on the deal and write what you are looking for: market, category, size.
  3. Say WHAT, never how many. A client signing fifteen may need to see hundreds.
  4. Talent get it on their day, with a roster already open and your brief on it.
Next: Sourcing runs while you are still negotiating, so names exist when they ask.
4

Send the quotation

Once Zain has checked it.
Go toClientsTo send
  1. You do not mint the link and you do not build the numbers. Zain does both.
  2. When it is checked it appears here and you are told.
  3. Copy the link into your OWN email. The client hears from you, as always.
  4. Then press "I have sent it" so it is on their record and not just your sent folder.
Next: The client opens it and picks. You see what they chose without asking.
5

Sign and hand over

When they say yes.
Go toClientsBrandsOpen the brand
  1. Get the agreement signed and uploaded against the brand.
  2. Hand over in writing: what was sold, at what price, by when.
  3. Introduce the account manager to the client yourself.
Next: Account management owns the client from here. You go and find the next one.

07 · Right and wrong

What good looks like

Everything on the right has actually happened here and cost us something.

What good looks like
  • Ask talent the moment a deal looks real, weeks before it closes.
  • Every deal has a next step and a date, or it is not being worked.
  • The budget goes on the deal as soon as you hear it, even if it is a range.
  • A lost deal keeps its reason. "They went with two brands only" is worth more than the deal was.
  • You send the client link yourself and then say you have sent it.
What has actually gone wrong
  • Waiting for signature before telling talent. This is the one that has cost us weeks on every campaign so far.
  • Telling talent how many creators to find. It reads as a target and they stop short.
  • Quoting a price that is not on the rate card without a founder saying so.
  • A board full of deals with no next step. It looks busy and tells you nothing.
  • Handing a client over in a conversation. If it is not written down, it did not happen.
08 · Vocabulary

Words we use

They mean something specific. A roster is not a list, and cost is not sell.

Brand
A company we are talking to. Becomes a client when a deal is won.
Client
A brand that has bought something. They have an account and can log in.
Deal
One conversation about money. A brand can have many, over years.
Campaign
A deal that was won and opened. Real creators, real money.
Roster
The creators sourced for one client. Has rounds, a brief and an owner.
List
A standing question: "fashion, UAE". Answers itself. Never client-specific.
Cost
What we pay a creator. Founders and talent only. Never shown to a brand.
Sell
What we charge a client. A creator with no sell price cannot be sold.
Quoted cost
What we assumed when pricing. Rarely what we end up paying.
Agreed rate
What a creator actually accepted. The only number that is real.
Request
Something somebody is waiting on, with an owner and a date.
Proposal
A priced roster sent to a client to choose from.
Share link
A /p/ link a client opens without logging in. Mint it, then send it.
Enrolment link
How a creator signs. They enter their own bank details; nobody types a fee.
The ladder
The rungs a booked creator climbs: briefed, content in, posted, paid.
Held
Stopped until a founder answers. Nothing has happened yet.
09 · The hard rules

Rules that cost money

Each one has already cost us something.

  • Never quote a price that is not on the rate card without a founder saying so.
  • You never see cost. Only what we sell for.
  • A handover said out loud is not a handover. Write it down.
  • An enquiry nobody has answered by tomorrow is a lost enquiry.

Every screen in the platform is explained under Guide in the menu. If something here does not match what you see, the product is right and this is out of date. Say so and it gets reprinted.
