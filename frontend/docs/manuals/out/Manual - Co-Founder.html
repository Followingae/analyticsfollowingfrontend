03 · Urgent or not

Clients will call many things urgent

Ask one question. The answer sets the reply time, and nothing else does.

The one question
Will a visit or a post suffer if this waits until 09:00 tomorrow?
A new request arrives in the client group. Whoever reads it answers it, then asks this.
Yes
Urgent
Drop planned work. Reply in 15 to 30 minutes.
  • A creator is at the store and something is wrong
  • A mistake on a post that is live
  • Tomorrow’s post or visit is blocked
  • A creator is unreachable on a live day
  • Wrong claim, wrong product, bad comments
No
Not urgent
Reply in 15 minutes with a time. Deliver within our times.
  • A new shortlist or more options
  • Scripts, changes, new ideas
  • Reports, insights, screenshots
  • Changes to next week’s plan
  • Invoices and payments, by email
Then log it, the same day

Every client request is raised on the platform as a Request the same day, whoever answers it. Answer the client first, as a person. Then log it, so somebody owns it and a date exists.

15 · The wall

The TV in the office

It shows what the platform knows, about a second after it knows it. Nobody writes on it.

What it shows
  • Every live campaign as a row: who is briefed, who has content in, who has posted.
  • Whose move it is, with the faces. Yours is lit when it is you.
  • A celebration when a client confirms, a post goes live or a deal is won.
  • Everything else floats up for ten seconds and is gone.
What puts it there
  • You do. A rung moved, a link pasted, a request closed, a rate agreed.
  • Nothing is typed onto the wall and nobody can push to it. There is no button.
  • A thing that happened in WhatsApp and nowhere else does not exist to the wall.
  • If the room should see it, record it. The wall moves the same second.
Never money
  • No fee, cost, margin or invoice on a team display.
  • Business development and the founders’ own work stay off it.
  • The wall is for the people delivering campaigns.
Pairing a TVThe TV opens following.ae/tv and shows a six-digit code. A staff phone enters it and picks the display. The TV remembers it through power off and on.

05 · Scout, continued

How each one works

ScoutOutreach finds them
  • One roster, one brief: location, follower range, language.
  • Everything happens at ScoutOutreach: a live bar, then a brand tile per campaign and its board left to right: Brief and fit, Reach, Review, Proposal. The roster keeps a ScoutOutreach tab with the funnel and an Open button; nothing is decided there.
  • Found creators wait in Reach for a yes or a skip before any DM. A founder can set a campaign to automatic. A barter campaign with no ceiling runs on a 100K default; no ceiling at all shows in red.
  • When ScoutAI is unsure how to answer, the draft waits in Reach beside the creator’s message: send, edit, or drop it and write yourself.
  • Screens on follower range, a post in 60 days, a real creator, not based elsewhere. Then an AI reads each against the brief and the team’s own yes and no, with a written reason.
  • A hashtag search reads the 40 most recent posts from up to 4 hashtags. Light analytics on each creator costs about $0.008.
  • The DM bot sends from the company’s Instagram, in Chrome on an office PC. No link: "Interested? Just reply here". ScoutAI holds the chat.
  • Creators who list an email get one automatically from hello@following.ae, with a WhatsApp button.
  • WhatsApp alerts go to creators in our network on Meta-approved templates. Paid is never called barter.
ScoutAI talks to them
  • A creator who replies to the DM talks to ScoutAI in that same Instagram chat, in English.
  • On WhatsApp, a campaign can run an open call with a code, like OC-D7F4E.
  • It explains the brief and asks the screening questions set for the campaign.
  • For paid work it asks their rate, and can counter within that campaign’s negotiation rules.
  • Creators answer with tap buttons or in their own words.
  • It adds them to the roster with their rate. A decline or a poor fit is recorded with the reason.
  • Its WhatsApp chats are kept on the Desk and never ring the team’s bell.

08 · Right and wrong

What good looks like

Everything on the right has actually happened here and cost us something.

What good looks like
  • Price what clients have already agreed to, before anything else in your day.
  • Clear rosters fast. Talent genuinely cannot move until you do.
  • Strike a creator with a reason written down. The reason stops the repeat next month.
  • Check the talent board when you are allocating, not only when something has gone wrong.
  • Use the quoted cost as a starting point, never as the answer.
What has actually gone wrong
  • Leaving a confirmed proposal unpriced. The client has said yes and is waiting on us.
  • Opening a campaign and agreeing rates afterwards. You are then negotiating with somebody who has already started working.
  • Approving a creator with no sell price. They cannot be sold, so they are not real.
  • Marking a payment paid before the transfer has actually left the bank.
  • Reading the talent board as a scoreboard. It shows load, not quality.
09 · The hard rules

Rules that cost money

Each one has already cost us something.

  • No payment goes out on work you have not confirmed landed.
  • Working capital is an amount you approve in writing, never a general permission.
