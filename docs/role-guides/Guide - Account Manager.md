# Account Manager — your guide

**Who this is for:** the person who owns the client once the deal is signed, runs their
campaigns, and keeps them informed. Today that is Sana.

**Your one sentence:** the client should never have to ask what is happening, and the team
should never have to ask what the client wants.

Everything below is a business step. The screen to do it on is in brackets.

---

## 1. Your day, in order

**First thing in the morning**

1. **Today** (`/work/today`). Your home screen. Work waiting on you, oldest first.
2. **Decisions** (`/work/inbox`). Anything needing an answer from you, from anywhere in the
   platform.
3. **Your clients** (`/work/clients`). Open the ones with live campaigns and look at where they
   are, before they email you.

**Through the day**

- Every campaign you run has a **Delivery board** (`/work/campaigns/[id]/ladder`) and a
  **Timeline**. Those are the truth. If you are answering a client from memory, open the board
  first.

**Before you log off**

- Any client who asked you something today has an answer or a date by which they will get one.

---

## 2. Taking over a new client

A client reaches you when business development has signed them.

**The steps**

1. **Get the handover in writing.** You need: what was sold, the budget, the number of
   creators, the deliverables per creator, the markets, the dates, who pays and when, and the
   client's point of contact. If one of those is missing, ask for it before you promise a date.
2. **Check the client exists in the platform** (`/work/clients`). If they do not, say so. A
   client who is only in your inbox is a client nobody else can help with.
3. **Introduce yourself to the client as their point of contact.** Short email, your name, your
   number, and what happens next and when.
4. **Open the campaign** (`/work/campaigns`, or `/work/campaigns/create`). One campaign per
   thing the client bought. Do not run two different pieces of work on one campaign because it
   is already open.

---

## 3. Briefing the team

This is the step that decides whether the whole campaign runs smoothly.

**A brief is not complete until it answers all of these**

1. Campaign name, and the client.
2. Timeline. Start date, and the last date a post can go live.
3. Objective and the main message, in one or two lines.
4. Deliverables per creator. Exactly: "1 reel", "5 to 7 stories", "1 reel plus 3 stories".
5. What the creator receives. Cash and how much, or barter and what value, or a voucher and
   what it covers, or reimbursement and up to what limit.
6. Market. UAE, KSA, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman.
7. Budget per creator, and how many creators.
8. Content rules. What must appear, what must not, hashtags, tags, whether the client approves
   before posting.
9. Whether this is paid, barter, or both. If both, say which rules apply to which.

**The steps**

1. Write the brief into the campaign in the platform, not only into an email. The brief travels
   with the campaign, so talent, leadership and you are all reading the same words.
2. The talent team see it on their Today. You do not need to forward it.
3. Answer their questions in the same place. A question answered in a private message is a
   question that gets asked again.

---

## 4. Getting the client to choose creators

1. Talent sources the creators onto the brand's **roster** (`/work/areas`).
2. Leadership reviews the list for quality and budget before the client ever sees it.
3. You send the client a **quote** (`/work/proposals`) or a share link (`/work/share`), not a
   spreadsheet. The client picks inside it, and their picks come straight back to you.
4. **Before anything goes to a client, check it for internal information.** Our cost prices,
   our margins and our internal notes are never on anything a client can open. The platform
   hides them for you. A spreadsheet does not.
5. When the client confirms, lock it (`/work/proposals/[id]/confirm`). That is what opens the
   campaign properly and stops the list drifting.

---

## 5. Running the campaign

1. Watch the **Delivery board** daily. It shows each creator and the one step they are on:
   rate agreed, contracted, briefed, content in, approved, posted, paid.
2. **Content approval.** If the client approves content before posting, send it to them through
   the platform and record their answer there. An approval that only exists in an email thread
   is an approval nobody else can find.
3. **When a creator drops out, tell the client the same day**, with what you are doing about it.
   Clients forgive a replacement. They do not forgive finding out late.
4. **Chasing** (`/work/chasing`) is the list of people who owe us something. Use it instead of
   remembering.
5. If something is stuck on a decision somebody else has to make, **nudge** it from Decisions.
   It emails them and they can answer without logging in, and it shows you how many times they
   have already been asked.

---

## 6. Reporting to the client

1. Reports come out of the platform (`/work/report-campaigns`). Open the campaign's report and
   send that.
2. Per creator we report: what they posted, when, and their own insights (reach, views,
   engagement) where we have them.
3. **Never invent a number.** If a creator has not sent insights, the report shows a dash. A
   dash is honest. A zero is a lie, because a zero means measured and nothing happened.
4. Send a status update on a schedule the client agreed to, not only when they chase you.

---

## 7. Money, your half of it

1. You confirm what the client owes and when: advance and balance, or on completion.
2. Invoices are raised by leadership. You tell them when a stage is complete so the invoice can
   go out, and you chase the client for the purchase order if one is needed.
3. **Chase payment before it is overdue**, not after. A polite note two days before the due
   date collects more than three notes after it.
4. Creator payments are not yours to approve or promise. If a creator asks you, point them at
   talent, or say it is with leadership.

---

## 8. Rules that do not bend

- **Our cost is never shown to a client.** You see sell prices. That is deliberate.
- **Nothing goes to a client that was not read by somebody first.** Especially a sheet.
- **A date you give a client is a promise from the whole company.** Check the board before you
  give one.
- **"We could not load this" is not "there is nothing here."** Read which sentence you got
  before you tell a client there is nothing.

---

## 9. Words we use

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Quote | What the client opens to pick creators and see prices. |
| Confirm | The client's yes, locked in, which opens the campaign. |
| Delivery board | Step by step board of every creator on one campaign. |
| Roster | A brand's sourcing list, round by round. |
| Barter | The creator is paid in product or voucher value, not cash. |
| Chasing | The list of people who owe us something now. |
| Report | The campaign's results, generated, not typed. |

---

## 10. If you are stuck

- Client asking for something that was not sold: say what was agreed, offer the nearest thing,
  and bring leadership in rather than agreeing on the spot.
- Talent has not come back with creators: check the roster first, then ask with a date on it.
- A decision is sitting with leadership: nudge it from Decisions.
- The platform is missing something you need to do your job: say exactly which screen and what
  is missing.

Inside the platform, **Show me how** (top right) runs a guided walkthrough on the real screen,
and **Guide** (`/work/guide`) explains what every screen you have is for.
