# Talent Manager — your guide

**Who this is for:** the person who finds creators, agrees their price, signs them, and gets the
content in. Today that is Aqsa.

**Your one sentence:** you turn a brand's request into real creators who have said yes, at a
price we can sell, with signed paperwork and content that lands on time.

Everything below is a business step. The screen to do it on is in brackets.

---

## 1. Your day, in order

**First thing in the morning**

1. Open **Today** (`/work/today`). This is your home screen. It shows only work that is
   waiting on you, oldest first.
2. Open **Decisions** (`/work/inbox`). Anything that needs you to answer, approve, or chase
   is listed here, pulled from every part of the platform at once.
3. Open **Pipeline** (`/work/pipeline`). This is every creator we owe something, across every
   brand, with the one thing that happens to them next. Red means late and nothing else.

**Through the day**

- Work from Pipeline, not from your memory and not from WhatsApp. Each row tells you the verb:
  "agree a rate", "send the agreement", "chase the post".
- When you move something forward, do it on the screen, not only in chat. If it only happened
  in WhatsApp, nobody else can see it and it will be asked about again.

**Before you log off**

- Nothing in Pipeline should be red without a reason somebody else can read. Either move it, or
  leave a note, or nudge the person who is blocking it.

## 2. Finding creators for a brand

This is your main job. It starts when a brief arrives and ends when the client has a list they
can choose from.

**The steps**

1. **Read the brief.** Market, category, budget per creator, deliverables (reel, story,
   carousel), paid or barter, and the dates. If any of those five are missing, ask before you
   source. Sourcing against a guess wastes a week.
2. **Open the brand's roster** (`/work/areas`). This is the brand's own list: what they asked
   for, who we found, who they turned down, and which round we are on. Everything you source
   goes here, not into a new spreadsheet.
3. **Search the master database first** (`/work/influencers`). We already know hundreds of
   creators with prices on them. Start there before you open Instagram.
4. **Add anyone new** (`/work/influencers/add`). Paste the handle. The platform pulls their
   followers, engagement and audience itself. You do not type those numbers in.
5. **Reach out and agree a price.** Ask for their rate per deliverable. Write down both numbers:
   their standard rate and what they actually agreed to. The agreed number is the one we use.
6. **Put the price on the creator** (`/work/influencers`, or the queue at
   `/work/influencers/review`). A creator with no price cannot be put on a quote at all. That
   queue is literally the list of creators nobody can sell yet, so keep it near empty.
7. **Build the list on the roster**, then hand it up for review.

**Who gets it next:** leadership reviews the list for quality and budget. Expect profiles to be
removed. That is normal and it is the point of the review.

**What gets a list sent back**

- Creators who are over the brief's budget.
- Creators the same brand has already used recently.
- Low engagement for the follower count.
- Creators who are not actually in the market the brief asked for. Check where they really live,
  not where their bio says.
- The same faces repeated across different campaigns.

Check those five yourself before you share. It saves a round trip every time.

## 3. Getting creators onto a campaign

Once the client has picked, each chosen creator has to actually be booked.

**For an app campaign (barter, paid deal, cashback)**

1. The campaign is set up in **App campaigns** (`/work/fa/campaigns`).
2. Share the **application link** with your creators. It looks like
   `creatorapp.following.ae/c/...`. They apply in the app, so their details, their address and
   their bank details arrive without you collecting them by message.
3. Approve or decline applicants on the campaign's funnel (`/work/fa/campaigns/[id]`).
4. Spots are counted on approved creators, not applicants. A full campaign means approved.

**For a paid campaign**

1. The creator appears on the campaign's **Delivery board** (`/work/campaigns/[id]/ladder`).
2. Move them along the steps: rate agreed, contracted, briefed, content in, approved, posted,
   paid. The board is the record. If a step has not been moved, as far as the company is
   concerned it has not happened.
3. **Give every row an owner.** Tick the creators and use "Give to". A list nobody owns is
   everybody's, which means nobody's.

## 4. Paperwork and products

1. **Agreements.** The platform sends the agreement and stores it signed. Do not collect signed
   PDFs by email and keep them in a folder. If a creator cannot sign through the link, say so,
   do not work around it quietly.
2. **Barter products and vouchers.** Never send a voucher or a product before the brand has
   confirmed in writing that you may. If you are not sure, you may not.
3. **If a creator pulls out after receiving the product**, say so the same day and arrange the
   collection. The brand needs to pick a replacement, and the product needs to come back.

---

## 5. Content and insights

1. Chase the post on the date it was due, not a week later (`/work/chasing`).
2. Content comes in through the platform and is reviewed there (`/work/fa/deliverables` for app
   campaigns, the campaign's Content screen for the rest).
3. If an edit is needed, **always write the note**. "An edit is needed" with no note means the
   creator does not know what to change and you will do the round twice.
4. **Insights screenshots.** Collect each creator's own Instagram insights and attach them to
   that creator (`/work/influencers`, the Insights tab). Brands open them from the creator in
   any quote. A creator with none simply shows no button, so there is no harm in a gap, only a
   missed sale.

---

## 6. Money

You prepare it. You do not release it.

1. Keep every amount we owe a creator in **Payables** (`/work/payables`). Not in a spreadsheet.
2. A payable has to be approved by leadership before it can be paid. You raise it, they approve.
3. Approved payables are paid in a **payout run** (`/work/payouts`), which is where the bank
   file comes from. A line with no bank details on file cannot go into a run, and the screen
   says so per line.
4. **If a payment is sitting and nobody has decided**, nudge. There is a Nudge button on the
   decision in Decisions and on the row in the delivery board. It emails the person who has to
   decide, and they can answer from the email without logging in. It can be used once a day
   per decision, and it tells you how many times they have already been asked.

**Never** tell a creator a payment is approved before it is. Tell them the date we expect, or
tell them it is with leadership.

---

## 7. Rules that do not bend

- **Our cost is never shown to a brand.** You can see what we pay a creator. The brand sees
  only what we charge. Never paste an internal rate sheet into a client email.
- **A creator with no sell price is not sellable.** The platform holds them inactive on purpose.
- **Exports are a last resort.** A spreadsheet that leaves the screen takes creator phone
  numbers, addresses and bank details with it, and none of the platform's rules travel with it.
- **A screen that says it could not read something is not good news.** "Nothing to show" and
  "we could not load this" are different sentences. Read which one you got.

---

## 8. Words we use

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Cost | What we pay the creator. |
| Sell | What we charge the client. |
| Barter | The creator is paid in product or voucher value, not cash. |
| Roster | A brand's own sourcing list, round by round. |
| Delivery board | The step by step board for one campaign's creators. |
| Pipeline | Every creator across every brand, with the next action. |
| Payable | An amount we owe one creator. |
| Payout run | A batch of approved payables, turned into a bank file. |

---

## 9. If you are stuck

- A decision is waiting on leadership: **nudge it** from Decisions.
- A brief is missing information: ask the account manager, in writing, and say what you cannot
  start without.
- A creator has gone quiet: log it on the row. Three attempts with dates beats "they are not
  replying".
- Something in the platform looks wrong: say exactly which screen and what you expected. "It is
  not working" cannot be fixed.

Inside the platform, **Show me how** (top right) runs a guided walkthrough on the real screen,
and **Guide** (`/work/guide`) explains what every screen you have is for.
